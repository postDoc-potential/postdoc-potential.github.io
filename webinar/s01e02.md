In this session, Dr Manmeet Singh shared his insights on collaboration, research and networking opportunities for people interested to work in climate science and Artificial Intelligence (AI). The key takeways are: 

- Collaboration develops around well-deifined problems, not simply around disciplines.
- Observational datasets when made accessible, well-structured, and AI-ready, can be valuable for AI research.
- Sharing research output through open-source code, application-ready products, publications is useful for building professional networks.
- Domain-specific conferences (e.g. AMS and AGU) and major AI conferences (e.g. ICML, ICLR, and CVPR ) are great opportunities for networking.
- Explore research opportunities beyond traditional academia, including public AI labs, technology companies, and interdisciplinary research groups.

- [ICML](https://icml.cc/) — International Conference on Machine Learning
- [ICLR](https://iclr.cc/) — International Conference on Learning Representations
- [CVPR](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/) — Conference on Computer Vision and Pattern Recognition

- [AGU Meeting](https://www.agu.org/meetings/all-meetings/agu27) — American Geophysical Union
- [NVIDIA GTC](https://www.nvidia.com/gtc/) — NVIDIA's AI and technology conference

##### About Speaker
Dr. Manmeet Singh leads the DSOC AI Research Lab as an Assistant Professor at Western Kentucky University. Originally from Delhi, India, he attended Rosary School and studied Civil Engineering at Thapar University (Patiala, Punjab) before earning his Ph.D. in Climate Studies from IIT Bombay. <br>
He previously served as a scientist at the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM) and as a Distinguished Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Texas at Austin. A Fulbright-Kalam Fellow, he brings together artificial intelligence, weather and climate science, and disaster resilience in his research. He enjoys collaborating across disciplines and helping students turn scientific ideas into practical tools.
