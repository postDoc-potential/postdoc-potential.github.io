#### _Next Step After Your PhD? Episode #2_

In this session, Dr Manmeet Singh shared his insights on collaboration, research and networking opportunities for people interested to work in climate science and AI. The key takeways are: 

- Collaboration develops around well-defined problems, not simply around disciplines.
Climate and Earth-science researchers have a major advantage because they understand problems that AI specialists may not yet recognize.
- Observational datasets are a major opportunity for AI, particularly when they can be made accessible, well-structured, and AI-ready.
- Share your research openly through code, tools, publications, conferences, workshops, and professional networks.
- Follow both domain-specific conferences, such as AMS and AGU, and major AI conferences, such as ICML, ICLR, and CVPR.
- Explore research opportunities beyond traditional academia, including AI labs, technology companies, and interdisciplinary research groups.

##### About Speaker
Dr. Manmeet Singh leads the DSOC AI Research Lab as an Assistant Professor at Western Kentucky University. Originally from Delhi, India, he attended Rosary School and studied Civil Engineering at Thapar University (Patiala, Punjab) before earning his Ph.D. in Climate Studies from IIT Bombay. <br>
He previously served as a scientist at the Indian Institute of Tropical Meteorology (IITM) and as a Distinguished Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Texas at Austin. A Fulbright-Kalam Fellow, he brings together artificial intelligence, weather and climate science, and disaster resilience in his research. He enjoys collaborating across disciplines and helping students turn scientific ideas into practical tools.
